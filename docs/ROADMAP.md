# Website roadmap

Written 2026-10-31. Internal. These are the items the owner listed for after the current redesign. Each has a trigger
(what must be true before work starts), the plan, and what could go wrong. Nothing here is built yet.

Order is by dependency, not by date. Items marked "waiting" need a decision or material from the owner.

## 1. Keep the app domain separate from the marketing domain

Trigger: the day the app needs any address of its own (activation, update checks, support, a download portal).

- The marketing site lives on the main domain (for example `example.com`). Anything the app talks to lives on another
  subdomain or another domain, for example `app.example.com` or a separate name. Do not add app endpoints to the
  marketing site, and do not add a `functions/` folder to it.
- Why: the marketing site has a strict content security policy, a static build and a free hosting plan. An app service
  has different security needs, different deploy timing and its own failure modes. A fault there must not take the
  public site down, and the public site's rules (for example `form-action 'none'`) must not have to loosen for it.
- The app domain needs its own Cloudflare Pages project or Worker, its own API token with one permission, and its own
  rules. It should not be indexed: serve `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` and a `robots.txt` that disallows everything.
- HSTS on the main domain uses `includeSubDomains`, so every subdomain must serve HTTPS before it is created. State
  that in the checklist for each new subdomain.
- Decision for the owner when this starts: one name with subdomains, or a second domain. A subdomain is cheaper and
  shares trust; a second domain gives complete separation of cookies and reputation.

## 2. Email from separate subdomains

Trigger: the first email the product or site must send (receipts are sent by the stores, so the first real one is
likely a support reply, a licence or activation message, or a newsletter).

- Never send product or marketing mail from the main domain. Spam complaints against one kind of mail would damage the
  address people use for every other kind. Use subdomains, for example `mail.<domain>` for transactional mail (activation,
  account notices), `news.<domain>` for any newsletter. The people-to-people company mailbox stays on the main domain.
- Provider: a transactional email service such as Resend. Resend's own advice is to send from a subdomain rather than the
  root domain, to add each subdomain as its own domain, and to keep open and click tracking off for transactional mail
  while allowing it for marketing mail. Its dashboard gives the exact DNS records for each subdomain.
  Check its free-tier cap and price on its pricing page before choosing, and compare one alternative.
- Records to publish, per sending subdomain, in Cloudflare DNS. Mail records must be DNS only (grey cloud):
  - SPF: a TXT record that names the provider as an allowed sender.
  - DKIM: the provider's signing key, as the TXT or CNAME records it shows. Use 2048-bit keys where offered. Google's
    sender rules ask for at least 1024 bits and recommend 2048.
  - DMARC: a TXT record at `_dmarc.<subdomain>`. Start with `p=none` and an address for reports, read the reports for a
    few weeks, then move to `quarantine`, then `reject`. Add one on the main domain too, so nobody can send as the main
    domain unnoticed.
- Gmail's rules (from 2024): all senders need SPF or DKIM, a spam rate under 0.3 percent (aim for under 0.10), and
  TLS. Senders of more than 5,000 messages a day also need DMARC and one-click unsubscribe on marketing mail. We will be
  far below that volume, but we set up all three from the start.
- Send only to people who asked for the mail. Do not buy lists. A bad batch costs the subdomain's reputation, which is
  exactly what the separation is for.
- Test before launch: send to a test Gmail and a test Outlook address, view the original headers, and check SPF, DKIM
  and DMARC all show pass and aligned. Use a tool that reads the headers rather than judging by inbox placement.
- Public contact address: publish it on `/contact/` only after the company mailbox exists (`src/content/copy.mjs`).
  Do not invent one.

## 3. Google indexing through the sitemap

Trigger: the custom domain is live. The full checklist is in `docs/OPERATIONS.md` section 5.

- The sitemap is generated by `scripts/build.mjs` and follows `OF_SITE_URL`, so it is correct the moment the site is
  built for the domain. Submit it in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
- With roughly 700 pages, most of them tutorials, expect Google to index in stages. Do not mass-request indexing; one
  request per key page, then let the sitemap work.
- Keep `lastmod` honest. Update it only when a page's content changes, not on every build, or Google will stop trusting it.
  Check the `sitemap.xml` generator when the owner reports that dates look wrong.
- Review monthly: the Pages report in Search Console for "Discovered, currently not indexed" and "Crawled, currently not
  indexed". Many thin tutorial pages with similar wording can land there. The response is better content on the
  important pages, not more submissions.

## 4. Hero animation (waiting)

Trigger: the owner reads `docs/decisions/hero-animation-style.md` and picks a direction.

- Recommended primary: Aurora Gradient, pure CSS, in the brand's coral, orange and amber over the site's navy and cream.
  Alternative: a pre-rendered Ink in Water loop. See the decision file for scores and sketches.
- Not started. Nothing in the hero changes until the owner chooses.
- Acceptance: the home page's Largest Contentful Paint does not get worse than the static hero, motion stops with the
  reduced-motion setting, there is a way to pause it, text contrast stays at least 4.5 to 1 in both website themes,
  and the product picture stays the first thing the eye lands on.

## 5. Real photography (waiting)

Trigger: the owner provides direction and material. Until then the site shows real app screens only.

- Needed from the owner: the kind of picture (people at work, sets, desks, scripts on paper), who may appear, and
  written permission from everyone who appears. Prefer real writers and crews over stock. Do not use AI-generated
  pictures of people on a site whose pitch is a tool for human writers, unless the owner decides otherwise.
- Technical plan: shoot or receive the largest size, resize at build time into two or three widths with `sharp`
  (AVIF and WebP, plus a JPEG fallback only if needed), write real `alt` text, set width and height to prevent layout
  shift, lazy-load below the fold, and keep each picture under about 150 KB on mobile. No paid image service.
- Watch the 20,000-file limit on the Free Pages plan (8,621 files on 2026-10-31). Each picture in three widths and
  two formats is six files.
- Add credits only where the licence requires them.

## 6. Testimonials and case studies (only when genuine)

Trigger: real users have used the product and agreed in writing.

- Never publish a quote, a name, a company, a number or a logo that was invented, paraphrased into something the person did
  not say, or taken without permission. The brief's rule against invented reviews and statistics applies here.
- A testimonial needs the person's written consent, their exact words, their name and role as they want it shown, and a
  date. Keep the consent email in the company records, not in the repository.
- A case study needs a real project, a real result the customer confirms, and the customer's approval of the final text.
  Do not publish the numbers as a promise of what others will get.
- Add `Review` or `AggregateRating` structured data only if the ratings are real, visible on the page, and come from
  a source the visitor can check. Google may remove rich results for markup that does not match visible content.
- Build the page template only when the first genuine one exists, so we never ship empty placeholders.

## 7. Changelog and release notes

Trigger: the first public release of the app, and the first store listing.

- One page, `/changelog/`, newest first. Each entry has a version, a date, and a short list of what changed written for
  a writer or filmmaker, not for a developer. Same rules as everything else: plain words, no em dashes, no internal
  technology names, no counts of tests or screens.
- Source of truth: a data file in `src/content/` (for example `changelog.mjs`), one object per release, so a build can also
  produce the feed and the sitemap entries. An optional Atom feed at `/changelog/feed.xml` costs almost nothing to
  generate and helps people follow along.
- Keep the page honest about fixes. Say what was wrong and what happens now.
- Use the same text for the store release notes where the stores allow it, so the two never disagree.
- For the website itself, keep a short internal log of notable changes in `docs/HANDOFF.md`. That is not public.
