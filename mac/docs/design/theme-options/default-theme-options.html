OpenFrame Mac: default theme

What is wrong with the current default
  • Sidebar, scene list and page area are the same flat grey, so nothing tells you where you are.
  • Orange is on almost every toolbar button in Dark, so the one thing that matters never stands out.
  • The scene list sits on a grey block that reads as disabled.
  • Light and Dark feel like two different apps (warm beige light, blue-black dark).

Every option below fixes these the same way: one quiet neutral family per mode, the accent only on the selection, the primary button, focus and links, and three clear layers (sidebar, list, page).

A. Native

Looks like a first-party Mac app: Finder and Notes greys, system blue accent. The safest choice; nothing will feel out of place next to other Mac apps.

  • Best for: people who want the app to disappear.
  • Risk: little OpenFrame identity.

B. Paper

Warm ivory and warm charcoal, a terracotta accent. Built for long writing sessions: softer than pure white, reads like good paper.

  • Best for: writers spending hours in the screenplay.
  • Risk: warm tint can look dated if overdone.

C. Screening Room

Cool slate neutrals with the OpenFrame amber kept as the accent, used sparingly. Closest to today's brand, without the noise.

  • Best for: keeping the brand colour, film-tool feel.
  • Risk: amber buttons need dark text, a little unusual on Mac.

D. Ink

Black and white with a single record-light red for the selection marker and links. Primary buttons are black in Light and white in Dark.

  • Best for: a sharp, editorial, modern look.
  • Risk: stark; status colours carry more weight.
