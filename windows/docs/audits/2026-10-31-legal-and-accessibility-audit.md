## 2. Legal and privacy (app side)

### 2.1 Data-flow inventory

Full facts with file and line references: [data-flow fact sheet](../store/data-flow-fact-sheet.md). The points that
change what the policy must say:

1. **The Mac licence server is a company-run service that receives personal data.** In the Mac App Store build the app
   posts Apple's signed `AppTransaction` and transaction records (JWS) to `/v1/mac/validate` at launch, when the lease
   ends (about 6 hours), after StoreKit changes and on wake (`crates/openframe-entitlement/src/mac/server.rs:64-76`,
   `mac/crates/openframe-ffi/src/store.rs:294,354`). The server logs one line per request with Apple original
   transaction ids and keeps IP addresses in memory for rate limiting (`services/licence-server/src/lib.rs:12`,
   `http.rs:11`, `ratelimit.rs:1`). Transaction ids are pseudonymous identifiers tied to a buyer, so this is likely
   personal data under the DPDP Act and the GDPR. ADR-0021's line "no data leaves your computer except what you choose
   to send" is not true for the Mac store build and must not be used in the policy as written.
2. **Windows Microsoft Store licence calls are not wired yet** (`apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/lib.rs:506-510`). The fact
   sheet describes the designed behaviour; re-check when they are wired.
3. **Hugging Face** receives IP address, time, file names and the app version (user agent) only when the person clicks
   Download OpenFrame AI (`crates/openframe-ai/src/fetch.rs:108-129`).
4. **Browser-style storage:** `localStorage` and `sessionStorage` hold 13 screen-preference keys on Windows; the Mac
   uses `UserDefaults`. No cookies. Local, functional, never sent: no consent banner is needed in the app.
5. **Telemetry:** none found in either app (fact sheet section 5).
6. **The privacy draft is stale:** old project folder (`Documents\OpenFrame\Projects` instead of the chosen library),
   Windows only, no Mac licence server (fact sheet section 6).

### 2.4 Which laws apply, and how far

An orientation for the lawyer, not advice. The apps collect no personal data on a server except the Mac licence check;
the company's other touchpoints (website, support email, the stores) are outside the app.

| Law | Applies? | Why, and what it would need |
|---|---|---|
| **India, DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025** | Partly. Not to project data processed only on the person's computer: the company never receives it and does not decide how it is processed. Likely yes to the Mac licence server (transaction ids, IP address), support email and the website. | Notice in plain language (the Privacy Policy), a lawful ground (consent or a legitimate use), security safeguards, breach notice, a grievance contact, deletion when the purpose ends. The Rules phase duties in; the lawyer confirms dates. |
| **India, IT Act 2000 and SPDI Rules 2011** | Likely yes while still in force: a body corporate handling personal information publishes a privacy policy. Section 43A falls away when the DPDP Act fully commences. The company handles no sensitive personal data (passwords, financial data). | Published privacy policy; reasonable security practices. Not an intermediary under the IT Rules 2021 for this product. |
| **India, Consumer Protection Act 2019 and E-Commerce Rules 2020** | The stores are the marketplace e-commerce entities; the company is a seller on them. Seller duties likely apply. | Lawyer to confirm which seller duties apply when sale and refund run inside Microsoft's and Apple's stores: legal name and address, grievance contact, no misleading claims, no fake reviews (none found). Also the 2023 dark-patterns guidelines: check the trial reminders and the lock (section 2.3). |
| **EU, GDPR** | Yes, narrowly: the company offers the app to people in the EU and processes their personal data on the Mac licence server. Not for project data kept on the device. | Article 13 notice, a lawful basis (likely performance of the contract), records of processing, a processor contract with the server host, and whether an EU representative (Article 27) is needed for occasional low-risk processing. |
| **EU, ePrivacy (storage on the device)** | The app's `localStorage`/`UserDefaults` preferences and reading Apple's on-device receipts are strictly needed for the service the person asked for. | No consent banner in the app. |
| **EU consumer law (14-day withdrawal for digital content)** | Yes for EU buyers, but the stores run checkout and refunds. | The website's Refund page must not contradict the stores' rules or statutory rights; it should point to each store's process. |
| **European Accessibility Act** | Probably not for this app (sold through third-party stores, not an e-commerce service the company runs); the company may also be a microenterprise. | Lawyer to confirm. The app aims at WCAG 2.2 AA regardless. |
| **USA, state privacy laws (California CCPA/CPRA and similar)** | Almost certainly below the thresholds (revenue, number of consumers, selling data). | An accurate privacy policy. California CalOPPA may still require a conspicuous privacy policy for an online service collecting identifiers: lawyer to confirm for the licence server. |
| **USA, COPPA** | No: general-audience tool, collects nothing from children. | The policy says so. |
| **USA, FTC Act (deceptive practices)** | Applies to claims. | Keep the "no credit card", trial and "nothing is deleted" claims true; they are true today. |
| **Gemma Terms of Use** | Applies to distributing the model download. | The Terms page may need Gemma's use restrictions passed to users (the licence audit session owns the details). |

## 3. Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA)

### 3.1 What was already in place

- Every icon-only button found has a name: a TypeScript syntax scan of all `.tsx` files found 5 unnamed buttons, all
  false positives (hidden submit buttons and a menu item named by its screen-reader text). The Mac scan found 0 among
  11 icon-only shapes.
- A global `:focus-visible` ring (`apps/desktop/src/styles/app.css:58`) and a forced-colours (Windows contrast themes)
  block that outlines every selected state (`app.css:90-96`).
- Theme contrast is enforced in code at 4.5:1 for the theme's own text pairs
  (`crates/openframe-application/src/modules/appearance/theme.rs:24,792`), on the Mac in `ThemeEngineTests.swift`.
- Clickable rows and cards either carry a keyboard path (an Open button, a menu with Open, Previous and Next change
  buttons, dnd-kit keyboard sensors with `tabIndex={0}`) or are double-click shortcuts for an action also in a menu.
  15 click handlers on non-button elements were checked by hand; none was keyboard-only missing.
- Windows end-to-end spec `tests/e2e/specs/96-keyboard-and-screen-reader.test.mjs` walks Tab through eight workspaces
  and reads the Windows UI Automation tree (what Narrator reads) for unnamed controls. Mac:
  `mac/apps/macos/Sources/OpenFrameE2EDriver/Scenarios/AccessibilityScenarios.swift` (unnamed controls, duplicate ids,
  keyboard reach, Light and Dark).
- The mandatory licence screens trap focus, move focus to each tutorial step's heading, and announce the trial chip in
  words for screen readers.
- Images: none without `alt` in the Windows interface.

## 5. Questions for a lawyer

1. **L1.** Under the DPDP Act and Rules, is the company a Data Fiduciary for the Mac licence check (Apple transaction
   ids, IP address)? Which lawful ground: consent at first launch, or a legitimate use for a service the person asked
   for? From which date do the Rules' duties apply?
2. **L2.** Do the SPDI Rules 2011 still require anything beyond a published privacy policy until the DPDP Act fully
   commences?
3. **L3.** As a seller inside Microsoft Store and the Mac App Store, which duties of the Consumer Protection
   (E-Commerce) Rules 2020 fall on the company (legal name, address, grievance officer, refund statement), and where
   must they appear (website, store listing, app)?
4. **L4.** GDPR: is an EU representative under Article 27 required for the licence server, or does the occasional,
   low-risk exception apply? Which lawful basis? What retention for the server's log line with transaction ids?
5. **L5.** Refund page: what wording sends buyers to each store's refund process without limiting statutory rights
   (Indian consumer law, EU 14-day withdrawal for digital content)?
6. **L6.** After the trial ends or a purchase is refunded, the app blocks opening and exporting projects (files stay on
   disk). Is this acceptable under Indian consumer law, the 2023 dark-patterns guidelines and EU consumer law, or must
   a read-only export stay available?
7. **L7.** Terms: do Gemma's Terms of Use require its use restrictions to be passed to end users in the Terms, and how?
8. **L8.** The data note and file safety tutorial recommend Google Drive by name for backups. Any issue with naming a
   third-party service in a paid product?
9. **L9.** Does CalOPPA or any US state law reach the licence server's transaction ids at the company's size?
10. **L10.** Is the European Accessibility Act relevant to a desktop app sold only through third-party stores?
