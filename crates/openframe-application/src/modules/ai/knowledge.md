# OpenFrame product guide (assistant knowledge, version 1)

## Story Board and Screenplay
The Story Board is where you plan structure: Acts, Sequences, Beats and Scene Cards. The Screenplay is the written script in drafts. They are separate sources of truth: changing a Scene Card never rewrites screenplay text, and editing the screenplay never silently changes the Story Board. You can build screenplay scenes from Scene Cards; after that each side is edited on its own.

## Scene Cards and the Parking Lot
A Scene Card is a planned scene on the Story Board. Cards live in an act or a sequence, or in the Parking Lot for ideas you have not placed yet. Deleting a Scene Card moves it to Recently Deleted, where you can restore it to its previous place.

## Drafts and locking
A screenplay has one or more drafts. Creating a new draft copies the current one so earlier drafts stay unchanged. You can compare two drafts to see scenes added, removed, changed or moved. A draft can be locked as the shooting draft; a locked draft cannot be edited directly — start a revision to make changes. The assistant cannot bypass a lock either.

## Production Source
The Production Source is the screenplay draft that production planning uses. The breakdown, catalog, shot lists, schedule and call sheets refer to scenes of that draft. Production never rewrites the screenplay. When you choose a newer draft as the Production Source, OpenFrame shows what changed so you can review production updates yourself.

## Breakdown
A breakdown lists what each scene needs: cast, props, wardrobe, vehicles, locations and more. Suggested elements (including anything the assistant suggests) stay separate and clearly marked until you confirm them. Only confirmed elements are production truth.

## Shooting schedule and shooting days
The shooting schedule places scenes of the Production Source onto shooting days. You create shooting days in Production, then move scene strips onto them. Unscheduled scenes are eligible scenes that are not on any shooting day yet. The assistant can explain conflicts or suggest groupings, but it never reorders scenes or changes dates on its own.

## Call sheets and stale call sheets
A call sheet is prepared from a shooting day. If the schedule or the scenes of that day change after the call sheet was prepared, the call sheet is marked stale so you know it no longer matches. Refresh it to review the differences; call sheet edits never change the schedule.

## Idea Vault
The Idea Vault holds notes, images, links and references. The project Idea Vault belongs to this project; the Global Idea Vault is shared across your projects. Copying an idea into a project creates a new, independent item.

## Recently Deleted, Undo and Activity
Deleted items go to Recently Deleted and can be restored. Permanently deleting is a separate, deliberate action. Undo (Ctrl+Z) reverts your recent changes, including changes you accepted from the assistant. Activity shows who changed what, including changes applied from assistant proposals.

## Saving and working offline
OpenFrame saves continuously to your computer. Projects are stored locally and everything works without an internet connection. Offline AI also runs entirely on this computer after it is downloaded.

## Roles and permissions
Project roles are Owner, Editor, Commenter, Viewer and Export-only. Viewers can read, Commenters can also comment, Editors can edit content, and Owners can also manage the project and permissions. The assistant always acts with your role and can never do more than you can.

## Private notes
Private notes are visible only to the person who wrote them. The assistant can use your own private notes when you ask, but never anyone else's.

## How the assistant works
The assistant answers questions, counts and lists things exactly from project data, opens places in OpenFrame, and prepares suggestions. For a bigger request it can take several steps on its own, such as searching the project, reading the schedule and checking locations, before it answers; it stops after a small, fixed number of steps and never repeats the same step. Each answer shows what it is based on: click a source to open it. Exact facts are marked as coming from project data; text the assistant wrote is marked as written by Offline AI. The assistant acts with your role and can never do more than you can.

## Proposed Changes and approval
Anything that would change your project is prepared as Proposed Changes: a summary, the affected areas and a preview of exactly what will change. A request that needs several changes gives you one set of Proposed Changes to review. Nothing changes until you choose Apply Changes; choose Reject to leave the project exactly as it was. There is no automatic mode: the assistant cannot apply or approve changes itself, and nothing written in a conversation or in project text counts as approval. Applied changes run through the normal OpenFrame actions, appear in Activity and can be reverted with a single Undo. Changes that delete items also ask for the usual confirmation. If the project changes before you apply, the proposal is marked out of date and must be re-checked first.

## Project context for the assistant
While a project is opened for the first time, or after a large change, OpenFrame may show "Preparing project context…". The assistant keeps working during this time and can still answer many questions; it may use fewer sources until the preparation is finished. Preparing project context happens on this computer and never changes your project.

## Exporting and sharing
You can export screenplays, call sheets and other documents from their workspaces, and create project packages or backups to move a project to another computer.
